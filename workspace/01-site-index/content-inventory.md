## 2. Markets

### California

**Slug:** california  
**Name:** California  
**Country:** United States  
**Currency:** USD  
**Intro:**  
California is terrain before it is anything else. Ridges, canyons and bluffs decide where a house can sit, and the light, fog-filtered in the north, hard and golden in the south, decides how it should open.

**Places Covered:**
- Los Angeles
- Malibu
- Beverly Hills
- Westside
- Orange County
- San Francisco Bay Area
- Wine Country
- Select architectural destinations

**Market Notes:**
- **Terrain:** Hillside lots, fire zones and coastal bluffs shape every plan. The best houses step with the land rather than flatten it.
- **Lineage:** Bay Region shingle and redwood, Case Study glass and steel, Spanish Colonial courtyards, Sea Ranch restraint.
- **Light:** Marine layer on the coast, clear heat inland. Overhangs, clerestories and deep eaves do the real work.
- **What we look for:** Original architects on record, views held not borrowed, indoor-outdoor rooms that are used.

**Neighborhoods & Guide Entries:**
1. Pacific Heights (bay-area): Edwardian and Classical Revival houses along the ridge, bay views between them.
2. Mill Valley (bay-area): Redwood canyons under Mount Tamalpais; shingle houses and mid-century hillside glass.
3. Los Altos Hills (bay-area): Oak-studded acreage above the Peninsula, with room for architecture to spread out.
4. Hollywood Hills (los-angeles): Cantilevered modernism on steep lots; the city laid out below.
5. Pasadena (los-angeles): Greene & Greene Craftsman, Arroyo Seco gardens, deep tree cover.
6. Laguna Beach (orange-county): Artists' cottages and headland houses above coves and tide pools.
7. La Jolla (la-jolla): Sandstone bluffs, a walkable village and houses stepped toward the sea.

**Buyer Needs:**  
Architectural provenance, view protection, fire and slope reports read before the first visit.

**Seller Needs:**  
A story that explains the house: the architect, the site, why it was built this way.

**Relocating Needs:**  
Guidance on microclimates, schools and commutes that differ sharply within a few miles.

**Service Offerings:**
- Fire and coastal zones: We note wildfire severity zones and Coastal Commission limits on every relevant home.
- Architect records: Original drawings and architect attribution checked where they exist.
- Photography: Shot in the light the house was designed for, often fog-soft morning or late gold.

**Regions:**
1. **Bay Area** (bay-area): Redwood shingle, Victorian rows and mid-century hillside houses. Fog shapes the day; microclimates change street to street. | Places: San Francisco, Marin, Peninsula, Silicon Valley, East Bay
2. **Los Angeles** (los-angeles): Neutra, Schindler and Lautner still set the standard. Canyon houses, courtyard Spanish and modern glass above the basin. | Places: Westside, Beverly Hills, Malibu, Hollywood Hills
3. **Orange County** (orange-county): Headlands and coves where a house is judged by what it frames of the Pacific, and how gently it sits on the bluff. | Places: Laguna Beach, Newport Beach, Corona del Mar
4. **La Jolla** (la-jolla): Sandstone bluffs, marine light and Irving Gill's white simplicity, still echoed along the coast. | Places: La Jolla, Del Mar, Rancho Santa Fe

### Florida

**Slug:** florida  
**Name:** Florida  
**Country:** United States  
**Currency:** USD  
**Intro:**  
Florida is water, shade and air. Houses here are built around the breeze and the storm season in equal measure: loggias, courtyards and deep roofs carrying as much weight as the rooms.

**Places Covered:**
- Miami
- Miami Beach
- Palm Beach
- Fort Lauderdale
- Naples
- Select significant properties

**Market Notes:**
- **Water:** Bay, canal, Gulf or ocean: frontage, depth and elevation matter more than square footage.
- **Lineage:** Addison Mizner's Mediterranean Revival, Miami Modern, Sarasota School, and a new generation of concrete pavilions.
- **Climate:** Hurricane codes, flood elevations and cross-ventilation shape every serious house built since 1992.
- **What we look for:** Shade used well, gardens given time to mature, and architecture that stays cool without apology.

**Neighborhoods & Guide Entries:**
1. Miami Beach (miami): Bayfront islands, MiMo landmarks and new concrete pavilions on the water.
2. Coconut Grove (miami): Miami's oldest canopy; bungalows and modern houses hidden under banyans.
3. Coral Gables (miami): Planned Mediterranean streets, strict design review, coral rock walls.
4. Palm Beach (palm-beach): Mizner and Wyeth estates behind hedges; an island that protects its look.
5. Jupiter Island (palm-beach): Quiet, low-lying, and among the most private stretches of the coast.
6. Port Royal (naples): Deep-water canals to the Gulf, with generous lots and sunset exposure.
7. Las Olas Isles (fort-lauderdale): Finger islands with ocean access from the back lawn.

**Buyer Needs:**  
Flood zone, elevation, seawall condition and insurance reviewed before the romance.

**Seller Needs:**  
Presentation that shows how the house lives in heat and light, not only its size.

**Seasonal Owner Needs:**  
Houses that close up well for summer and open easily in winter.

**Service Offerings:**
- Storm-ready details: Impact glass, roof age and post-1992 code compliance noted on every home.
- Water access: Dock depth, bridge clearance and time to open water recorded where relevant.
- Historic review: Palm Beach and Coral Gables landmark status flagged up front.

**Regions:**
1. **Miami** (miami): MiMo on the Beach, Coral Gables' Mediterranean streets, the old tree canopy of Coconut Grove. | Places: Miami, Miami Beach, Coconut Grove, Coral Gables
2. **Palm Beach** (palm-beach): Mizner and Wyeth, clipped ficus hedges and a town that guards its architecture closely. | Places: Palm Beach, Manalapan, Jupiter Island
3. **Naples** (naples): Gulf sunsets, low horizons and deep-water canals in Port Royal and Aqualane Shores. | Places: Old Naples, Port Royal, Aqualane Shores
4. **Fort Lauderdale** (fort-lauderdale): The Venice of America: canal-front pavilions where the living room and the dock are one. | Places: Fort Lauderdale, Las Olas Isles, Boca Raton

### New York

**Slug:** new-york  
**Name:** New York  
**Country:** United States  
**Currency:** USD  
**Intro:**  
New York is history held in brick, brownstone and cast iron. Beyond the city: shingle, fieldstone and open field. Landmarks law, the block and the season shape everything.

**Places Covered:**
- Manhattan
- Brooklyn
- The Hamptons
- Hudson Valley
- Select Upstate properties

**Market Notes:**
- **The block:** Width, light and the street matter as much as the house. A 25-foot townhouse on the right block is its own category.
- **Lineage:** Federal and Greek Revival rows, Italianate brownstone, SoHo cast iron, Shingle Style on the East End, Dutch stone upstate.
- **Landmarks:** Historic districts protect the fabric and set the terms for any change. We note designation on every house.
- **What we look for:** Original detail intact, careful restoration over renovation, and light found in a dense city.

**Neighborhoods & Guide Entries:**
1. West Village (manhattan): Crooked streets, Federal and Greek Revival townhouses, the city at its most intimate.
2. Tribeca (manhattan): Cast-iron and warehouse lofts on cobblestone, generous volumes and light.
3. Upper East Side (manhattan): Limestone townhouses and pre-war co-ops between Central Park and Madison.
4. Brooklyn Heights (brooklyn): New York's first historic district; brownstones one block from the harbour.
5. Park Slope (brooklyn): Romanesque and Queen Anne rows beside Prospect Park.
6. Sagaponack (the-hamptons): Farmland meeting the ocean; shingle houses behind tall privet.
7. Rhinebeck (hudson-valley): River estates, stone farmhouses and a village that keeps its scale.

**Buyer Needs:**  
Co-op boards, landmark rules and townhouse condition explained clearly and early.

**Seller Needs:**  
Original detail given its due: mouldings, stoops, cast iron, before square footage.

**Country House Needs:**  
Weekend-ready homes within reach of the city, with land and privacy.

**Service Offerings:**
- Landmark status: Historic district and individual designation noted on every listing.
- Co-op and condo: Building type, board expectations and financing limits summarised simply.
- Seasons: East End and Hudson Valley homes photographed in the season that suits them.

**Regions:**
1. **Manhattan** (manhattan): Village townhouses off the grid, Tribeca lofts behind cast iron, and limestone on the Upper East Side. | Places: West Village, Tribeca, Upper East Side
2. **Brooklyn** (brooklyn): Brownstone and limestone rows with stoops, gardens and parlour floors largely intact. | Places: Brooklyn Heights, Cobble Hill, Park Slope
3. **The Hamptons** (the-hamptons): Silvered shingle, privet and farmland meeting the Atlantic dunes on the South Fork. | Places: Sagaponack, East Hampton, Southampton
4. **Hudson Valley** (hudson-valley): River estates, Dutch stone farmhouses and new barns set against the Catskills. | Places: Rhinebeck, Hudson, Millbrook, Select Upstate

## 5. FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| What is Matter of Place? | An editorial real-estate media platform for exceptional existing residential property in California, New York and Florida. |
| How does a property get featured? | We find it, or it's sent to us. We research the house, write the story and choose the photographs. Few make it. |
| What are you looking for? | A reason to stop. A named architect, a considered interior, a house true to its place. Price is not the filter. |
| Can I submit a property? | Yes. We reply either way, and a no is not a verdict on the home. |
| Do I have to pay to submit a property? | No. Submission is free. Exposure products apply only after a property is accepted; payment does not override selection. |
| What if my property is not accepted? | Nothing is charged. With Five Features, a declined property does not use a credit. |
| What does it cost? | The Feature $295, The Reach $695, The Campaign $1,495, or Five Features for $1,250. Programmatic media is billed separately. |
| Who receives my inquiry? | The representative named on the property, directly. We never sell or share your details. |
| Are you a brokerage? | No. We represent no one in a sale and guarantee no buyers. Every property credits its representative. |
| Where do you cover? | California, New York and Florida only. We do not currently cover new developments. |
| Is there a newsletter? | Place Notes: selected properties and stories, sent when there is something worth sending. |
| How accurate are the details? | Specifications come from the listing; history comes from our research. Spot an error? Tell us. |
