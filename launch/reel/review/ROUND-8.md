Three worst shots
1. 9.0 to 10.6 s, the grid: six tiles sit motionless on black with the bottom 480 px empty, and the tile then grows to full frame without a sound. Keep a slow push inside every tile, cut the hold to about 0.6 s, centre or fill the grid, and put a cue on the grow.
2. 14.5 to 18.0 s, the end card: 3.5 s of locked type over a barely moving wall, with the wordmark and the URL set on busy board-formed concrete. Shorten the hold to about 2 s and move the lockup onto the quiet sky, or onto an even plane of the wall, with a final tracking settle so it does not read as a freeze.
3. 5.7 to 8.1 s, the redwood price shot (the oak opener at 0 to 2.3 s has the same fault): one flat photograph scaled, with the price arriving like a caption over it. Split it into planes (deck, trunk, bay and sky) with different parallax, or add a rack focus, so the camera is inside the place.

The grid mixes a palm-and-pool tile and a classical colonnade with a Los Altos Hills label. I did not score the stock stand-ins, but the template should require that every tile comes from the same listing.
The headline sits over the sofa's bright cushions at 3.3 to 5.0 s and stays readable only because of the scrim. Check it against lighter interiors.
The flatness margin of 0.004 means a slightly more tonal bed or impact would fail the pitched-sound check. Give it headroom.
