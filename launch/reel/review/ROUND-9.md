Three worst shots
1. 14.0 to 18.0 s, wall and lockup: the camera nearly stops (difference 0.4 to 0.6 per frame) and the lockup holds for about 2.5 s on a still frame. Keep a slow crane or dolly running under the lockup through the last frame and cut the hold after the lock to about one second.
2. 8.75 to 10.3 s, grid: the six tiles settle and then sit almost motionless for 0.7 s on an Obsidian field, like a catalog page. Give each tile its own small drift and start the grow as the last tile lands, so the grid never rests.
3. 3.2 to 4.0 s, interior headline: the opacity dips to about 70 percent at 3.77 s and recovers, which reads as a flicker rather than a weight shift. Make the 400 to 500 crossfade keep the combined opacity at full, or drop the weight shift on this line.

- Frame 0 is a single Obsidian frame, then the oak photograph pops in at full brightness on frame 1. A platform thumbnail taken from the first frame would be black; open on the picture or fade up over at least 6 frames.
- The cut from the interior to the deck at 5.2 s is a straight cut, not a cut through black. It carries a whoosh but nothing in the picture motivates it.
