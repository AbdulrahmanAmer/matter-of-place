Three worst shots
1. **12.3 to 18.0 s, coastal wall and wordmark lock.** After 14.3 s the frame is close to still for 3.7 s, and the wall's vertical edge (x ≈ 225) touches the M of MATTER, so the two lines meet awkwardly. Keep a slow crane or depth dolly running through the lock and silence, and reframe so the wordmark sits fully on the wall or fully on the sky.
2. **10.5 to 12.3 s, colonnade growing out of the grid.** It is enlarged until soft, rides for about 1.3 s with no type and no event, and then hard-cuts away. Give it a reason (a rack focus onto the sea opening, or one line of type), or grow the tile straight into the closing shot.
3. **5.4 to 8.1 s, redwood deck with price and facts.** The push is flat and the price block sits still at the lower left over the underside of the deck. Split the photograph into planes (trunk, deck, bay), and either let the facts drift with the deck plane or settle the price with a weight shift.

The grid at 8.75 s mixes plainly different homes (Florida palms, an ocean bedroom) into a reel captioned Los Altos Hills. The fixture should use one property's rooms, so the next review judges the edit rather than the stand-ins.
