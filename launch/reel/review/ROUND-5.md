Three worst shots
1. 5.3 to 8.1 s (deck and redwood, facts row): the cut in is scored with a door that belongs to the emblem, the cut lands a few frames after the 5.3 s cue (still the sofa at 5.37 s), and the picture's brightness pumps with the facts row. Replace the door with a stone footstep or a short whoosh placed on the actual cut frame, and hold the scrim at one level for the whole shot.
2. 12.3 to 15.1 s (vertical split, price, emblem start): the price masks in over the wall edge in silence, sits as the climax, and the emblem starts assembling on the same photograph with no sound until the impact. Move the price next to the facts row in shot 3, or give it a paper cue and a shorter hold, and give the emblem planes their door thud at the start of the assembly.
3. 8.75 to 11.0 s (grid and tile grow): six tiles of unrelated colour temperature sit in the upper two thirds over 450 px of empty black, and the growing tile covers its neighbours as a flat rectangle. Apply one grade across the tiles, centre the grid in the safe area, and let the neighbours recede or dim as the chosen tile grows.

- The emblem on the end card shows a tonal ramp inside each plane (14.8 s and 16.7 s). If that is not the flat two-tone mark from the brand files, it breaks the no-gradient rule.
- On the deck fascia near y 1320 there are one or two stray light pixels at 5.73 s and 8.0 s, before and after the facts row. Pixel values could not confirm whether they are in the photograph or a leak from the parked type, so check that mask.
- The headline's second line crosses the brightest part of the sofa at 4.0 to 5.3 s, where contrast is the weakest in the reel.
